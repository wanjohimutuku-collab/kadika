# SEO and AI-citation plan: Kenya and Tanzania

Written 2026-10-15. Product framing: invitations, RSVP, michango (contributions) and gate check-in for
weddings, send-offs, engagement parties and other celebrations (ADR 024).

## Read this first: what is proven and what is not

- **No search volumes.** My search tool returns web pages, not keyword data. Every keyword below is a
  hypothesis from reading the market. Nothing here promises position 1; no one can. Validate each
  cluster with Google Search Console, Google Keyword Planner (set to Kenya and Tanzania, Swahili and
  English) and Google Trends before spending on content. Section 6 says how.
- **What the research did show:**
  - Google has about 99% of mobile search in Tanzania (Statcounter). Kenya figures were not found.
    Optimise for Google first; Bing matters for ChatGPT (see section 5).
  - **Tanzania already has local digital-card competitors:** Envaita, Ekadi and M-Kadi. Envaita has had
    press in The Citizen and The Guardian, and says its biggest problem is guests thinking the card is a
    scam. We already answer that with the verified-sender strip, so trust is a content angle.
  - **Kenya has no local digital-invitation product that I could find** with RSVP, QR gate and M-Pesa.
    Contributions are served by M-Changa, OneKitty and Changisha, which are fundraising tools, not
    invitation tools. Kenya is the bigger opening.
  - **Swahili wording searches are poorly served.** A search for card wording in Swahili returned
    printing listings, a forum thread and a newspaper column, with no page that answers the question.
    One query only, so treat it as a lead.
  - **Kitchen party and send-off are Tanzanian culture terms** with explainer coverage in The Citizen but
    no tool pages. Kenyan wedding money talk is about harambee, WhatsApp committees and fundraiser
    fatigue, which is where michango content can win.

Use hreflang (`sw-TZ`, `sw-KE`, `en-KE`, `en-TZ`) with separate Kenya and Tanzania landing pages. Do not

## 3. Content to publish (first 90 days)

Hub pages (each answers one question in the first 60 words, then goes deep):

1. `/ke/digital-wedding-invitations-kenya` — product page for Kenya (KES, M-Pesa, WhatsApp, QR gate).
2. `/tz/kadi-ya-harusi-mtandaoni` — same for Tanzania in Swahili (TZS, Mixx by Yas, HaloPesa).
3. `/michango-ya-harusi` and `/ke/wedding-contributions-m-pesa` — how to run michango with a committee,
   with a free pledge tracker explainer. Positions us beside M-Changa and OneKitty without attacking them.
4. `/send-off-invitation-tanzania` and `/kadi-ya-uchumba` — culture explainer plus wording, then the tool.
5. `/maneno-ya-kuandika-kwenye-kadi-ya-harusi` — 20 Swahili wordings by religion and tone (native-speaker
   review required), each with a "make this card" button. Also an English "wedding invitation wording
   Kenya" page.
6. `/trust/is-a-digital-invitation-safe` and Swahili twin — what a genuine card looks like (sender name,
   domain, committee phone, no attachments, never asks for OTP). Directly answers Envaita's scam problem.
7. `/gate-check-in-guide` — offline QR vs printed code vs name search; what to do when a QR fails.
8. `/compare/` — honest comparison tables: printed cards vs WhatsApp image vs Harusi Pass; M-Changa and
   OneKitty vs pledge tracking inside an invitation.
9. `/guides/wedding-cost-tanzania` and `/guides/wedding-cost-kenya` — original numbers are what get
   cited. Collect real figures from pilot weddings (aggregated, anonymised) and publish them.

Format rules for every page: one H1, question-style H2s, a direct answer in the first sentence or two
under each H2, a table or numbered list where it fits, a short FAQ at the bottom, author and reviewer
line, "last updated" date, and TZS/KES prices stated in text.

Interactive pages that earn links: free wedding budget calculator (KES and TZS), free printable pledge
sheet, RSVP wording generator, QR print-size checker. Each needs a plain-HTML explanation so search
engines and AI can read it.

## 4. Technical SEO for this codebase

Current state of `apps/web`: no `robots.txt`, no sitemap, no `metadataBase`, no hreflang. `/c/*` and
`/k` already carry noindex, which is correct and must stay (they are private guest cards).

1. Add `app/robots.ts`: allow `/`, `/ke`, `/tz`, guides; disallow `/app`, `/ops`, `/c/`, `/s/`, `/k`,
   `/dev`, `/api`. Allow AI crawlers you want citations from (OAI-SearchBot, GPTBot, PerplexityBot,
   ClaudeBot, Google-Extended); check each vendor's current user-agent names before publishing.
2. Add `app/sitemap.ts` listing public pages with `lastModified`; submit to Search Console and Bing
   Webmaster Tools (ChatGPT retrieval overlaps with Bing results).
3. Set `metadataBase`, canonical URLs and `alternates.languages` for the four locale pages.
4. JSON-LD: `Organization` (name, logo, phone, sameAs), `SoftwareApplication` (offers in KES and TZS),
   `Article` with `dateModified` on guides, `BreadcrumbList`. `FAQPage` is optional; Google has
   narrowed FAQ rich results, so add it for answer structure, not for a rich result.
5. Performance: the public pages must hit LCP under 2.5 s on slow 4G (same budget as the card). Server
   render all copy; no content behind client-only JS.
6. Home page copy today is a single hero and sections; add a visible "who sends this, how we verify" block
   and the three event types so the page matches the queries above.
7. Pick the domain layout once: `harusipass.co.tz` plus `.co.ke`, or one domain with `/tz` and `/ke`.
   One domain with folders concentrates links and is simpler to start. Decide before publishing.
8. Product name: the brand "Harusi Pass" says wedding. If Send-off and other events matter, own the
   phrase "Harusi Pass for send-offs and engagements" in titles, or rename (open decision).

## 5. Getting cited by AI answers (GEO)

What the sources agree on (mostly vendor blogs, so treat as guidance, not law):

- Put the answer in the first sentence under each heading; models lift passages, not pages.
- Rank in Google first (AI Overviews favour pages that already rank). ChatGPT retrieval overlaps heavily
  with Bing, so verify Bing indexing.
- Tables, lists and definitions are easier to quote. Original data and named sources get cited.
- Freshness: update dates on guides and re-publish figures yearly.
- Entities: be consistent about the company name, phone and domain everywhere; get listed on Wikidata
  (if notability allows), Crunchbase, LinkedIn, Google Business Profile, and local tech directories.
- Earn mentions where AI looks: pitch The Citizen, The Guardian (Tanzania), Techcabal, Nation, Tuko and
  Kenyan wedding blogs with an original-data story (wedding cost report, % of guests on feature phones).
- Check what AI says today: ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude the 20 questions in cluster D
  and E monthly, log whether we are cited and who is.
- `llms.txt` is cheap but unproven; add one only as an extra.

## 6. Validate and measure

1. Keyword Planner: enter the sw and en terms above for Kenya and Tanzania; keep terms with real
   volume; note KES/TZS price ideas. Check Google Trends for wedding-season peaks (plan content 8 weeks
   ahead of December and school holiday peaks).
2. Search Console: after 4 to 6 weeks, find queries we already show for and strengthen those pages.
3. Look at the live SERPs for each A and B term and write down who ranks (Envaita, Ekadi, M-Kadi,
   M-Changa, OneKitty, Jiji printers, Jamii Forums). Beat the weakest result first.
4. KPIs: impressions and clicks per cluster and country; non-brand sign-ups; AI referral sessions
   (chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini, claude.ai); citation count in the monthly prompt test.
5. Realistic expectation: low-competition Swahili and Kenyan long-tail terms can reach page one within
   a few months of consistent publishing; head terms like "digital wedding invitation" take longer and
   depend on links and brand mentions.
